Full description

UniBE is interconnected. With us, you're part of an international community and benefit from interdisciplinary collaboration.

Tasks

  • Conducting literature reviews and preliminary analyses to support academic publications and ongoing international research projects
  • Assisting with the planning, preparation, and organization of academic events, including seminars, conferences, and lectures (both online and in person)
  • Reviewing and updating teaching materials

Qualifications

  • Advanced undergraduate studies, ideally with a completed Bachelor's degree in law and/or the social sciences
  • Interest in interdisciplinary academic research
  • Interest in contemporary issues in environmental and climate law and related socio-political debates
  • Excellent written communication skills in German and English (ideally both at C1 level)
  • Strong critical thinking and analytical skills
  • A precise and meticulous approach to work
  • Advanced proficiency in Microsoft Word and PowerPoint
  • Willingness to work flexibly within the agreed working hours, including varying workloads throughout the year
  • Ideally, pursuing a Master's degree with a focus on climate and/or environmental law or climate policy

Collaborative environment and ambitious team Application and Contact Please submit your application, including a one-page cover letter (in English), a detailed CV, degree certificates and academic transcripts, employment references, and proof of current university enrolment, by email to Marlen Weibel (marlen.weibel@unibe.ch) no later than 18 December 2026. Interviews will take place in early January 2027. With us, you create value. For yourself and for society.

About us

The Climate Law and Society Group, led by Prof. Dr. Laura Mai at the Institute of Public Law, is looking for a Student Research Assistant to join its team. You will work closely with Prof. Dr. Laura Mai and her team, gaining hands-on experience in academic research at the intersection of climate law and the social sciences. We offer flexible working hours, a varied range of tasks, and opportunities to contribute to interdisciplinary research in an international academic environment.

Do I need a cross-border work permit for a role in Bern?

EU/EFTA citizens living in an EU/EFTA state can apply for a G permit and can work anywhere in Switzerland: border zones no longer apply to them, and they must return home at least once a week. The Swiss employer files the request with Canton Bern's migration office after the contract is signed. Processing times vary by canton, so confirm the specifics with Bern's cantonal migration office or with HR during the application.
